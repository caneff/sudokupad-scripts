# sudokupad-scripts

Tampermonkey userscripts for [SudokuPad](https://sudokupad.app).

## Install

Install [Tampermonkey](https://www.tampermonkey.net/), then open a script's link below. Tampermonkey offers to install it.

- [Color by Digit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caneff/sudokupad-scripts/main/color-by-digit.user.js): adds a button that colors every cell holding a digit, and every empty cell whose centre marks all belong to one group, using groups of palette colors set in a settings dialog.
- [Digit Color](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caneff/sudokupad-scripts/main/digit-color.user.js): colors the digits themselves, one color per digit, in entered digits, corner marks and centre marks. Starts from default colors; change them in a settings dialog. Givens keep their color; a conflicting mark keeps its digit color and gets a small red circle behind it.
- [Pen Tool Extras](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caneff/sudokupad-scripts/main/pen-tool-extras.user.js): six things for the pen tool: the colors, the pen's line mode and the edge and corner marks in one settings dialog, a second and a third page of nine pens, and two sliders in the pen tool's own panel. **Colors:** changes the pen's nine colors. Strokes already drawn and the tool's color buttons change with them. With nothing saved, the nine pen colors are your current cell color page, darkened, each pen on the same button number as its cell color (pen 1 takes the page's color on button 1, and so on; button 0 is not used), with the darkest grey turned black, so installing changes the pen colors straight away; a cell color that is transparent or unreadable leaves SudokuPad's own pen color on that button; edit and Save to keep your own. In the dialog the nine colors are a 3 by 3 grid in the arrangement of the pen tool's buttons (1 2 3, 4 5 6, 7 8 9), each picker with its number. Holding a pen color button for a second (with either mouse button) puts a color picker over each button, as the cell color tool does: click one to open the picker, and the pen changes live and the set is saved; hold again, or leave the pen tool, to close them. The colors are read when the page loads, so changing cell colors later does not move the pen until the next load. **Thickness:** one slider (1 to 16; SudokuPad's own pen line is 5) scales pen lines, the pen's cell circles and crosses, and the edge and corner marks together: at 5 everything draws at its usual width, and moving the slider changes each by the same factor. Edge marks and corner marks (the edge X, the arrows and circles, the corner X and circle) also grow in length above 5, evenly to 1.3 times at 16, centred on their edge or corner, with the same stroke width as before the growth; lines do not change. The growth is a CSS scale, so marks already drawn grow too. Dark Page keeps the largest of them inside its rectangle. The slider sits in the pen tool's panel, so it changes during play and exists only while the pen tool is on: SudokuPad's line mode pulldown is hidden (its choice moved to the settings dialog, see Line mode below) and the two sliders take its row, Thickness above Opacity, each with a small line icon, beside the page button of Pen pages. A move changes the marks live and is saved at once under `PenColor_thickness`, with no Save or Cancel; it does not turn the colors into a saved set, adds nothing to undo or the replay, and gives the keyboard back to the puzzle. **Slider options:** the settings dialog has a Sliders section: Show thickness slider and Show opacity slider (both ticked by default; saved under `PenColor_sliders`, so settings saved without it keep both showing) and the Thickness and Opacity sliders themselves, which are always there, hidden from the panel or not. A hidden slider's value still applies to the marks and can still be changed in the dialog. With one slider hidden the other keeps its place in the panel; with both hidden the panel's last row holds only the page button. A dialog slider, like the rest of the dialog, takes effect on Save and Cancel leaves it; the panel's sliders keep saving at once, and the dialog opens at the value in force, so the two show the same value. **Opacity:** a second slider (20 to 100 percent, in steps of 5) sets how see-through every pen mark is: lines, cell circles and crosses, edge marks and corner marks. SudokuPad draws pen marks at 80 percent (`.cell-pen { opacity: 0.8 }` in `tool-pen.css`), so 80 is the default and leaves a single stroke as it is (overlaps differ, as described next); 100 is solid, and 20 is the floor where a mark still shows, so dense marks stop hiding the digits underneath. It is the second slider in the pen tool's panel, changes the marks live and is saved at once under `PenColor_opacity`. The opacity is set on the whole pen layer, not on each stroke, so strokes that overlap (the segments of one line, or two lines that cross) show the same as the rest of their stroke and no longer darken at the joins; where two colors cross, the later stroke covers the earlier one instead of blending with it. The drag highlight and the erase highlight keep SudokuPad's own look at every slider value. **Pen pages:** a second page of nine pens, and a third (below), for 27, paged the way SudokuPad's cell color tool pages its colors. Page 2 draws the same nine colors as page 1 with dotted lines (a free line, an edge line or a diagonal; the cell circle, the cross and the edge and corner marks stay solid), and the dots scale with the Thickness slider. The dialog's Dash length slider (0 to 8 line widths, saved under `PenColor_dash`) turns each dot into a dash: 0 is the dots of today, which saved settings without it keep, and a dash is that many line widths long, so it scales with Thickness, changes the page 2 lines already drawn, and shows on the page button; each cell's piece of a line starts and ends with half a gap, so dashes are the same length across a cell boundary, and a dash too long for a cell to hold a gap is shortened to leave one, so a page 2 line is never solid. The page 2 swatches stay dots. A page button at the right end of the panel's last row shows the line the next page draws, with a dot for each page and the current one filled; click it, press Tab, or turn the mouse wheel over the pen buttons (down for the next page, up for the previous) to change page. The same pen number stays selected, so Tab turns a green line into a dotted green one. The selected pen, page included, is saved under `PenColor_pen` and comes back after a reload; SudokuPad's own `ToolPen_color` only ever holds the pen's number, so removing this script leaves stock SudokuPad working with that pen on its first page. Recoloring a pen, in the dialog or by holding its button, changes it on every page. Tab does nothing else in the pen tool with this script on, with No Tab Letters installed or not (Shift+Tab is left to SudokuPad). **Chevron lines (page 3):** a third page of nine pens, the same nine colors, draws a thin line with a run of chevrons along it, pointing the way you dragged. A swatch on this page shows a chevron, and the page button shows one while the next page is this one. Only lines are chevron lines (a free, edge or diagonal line, each cell's piece drawn with whole chevrons so a run reads as one across a cell boundary); a cell circle, a cross, and the edge and corner marks stay solid. The chevrons scale with Thickness from 5 up (below 5 they keep their size at 5, so the direction stays visible, and the thin line under them gets thinner only down to a minimum that still shows, with sparser chevrons, about three to a cell at 5; they are drawn again when it changes, so lines already drawn follow, and they stay inside the rectangle Dark Page keeps at 16) and follow Opacity. A pen value has no direction, so a page 3 pen is two color characters, `j` to `z` and `0`, two to a pen: a line stores the one for the way each segment was dragged, so a drag that turns stores the right character per segment and is still one undo step, and the replay and a reload keep it. Dragging over a line erases it whichever way either was drawn. Recoloring a pen changes it on all three pages. **Lines drawn with a page 3 pen, like page 2's, are invisible to anyone without this script**; **Lines drawn with a page 2 pen are invisible to anyone without this script**: SudokuPad stores them and shares them in the replay, but without this script's CSS it draws them with no color, so a viewer without the script sees the marks missing. **Line mode:** the dialog's Pen line mode (Center & Edge, Center only, Edge only) is SudokuPad's own setting, saved where its pulldown saved it; Save applies it. **Edge marks:** adds direction arrows (`>` `<` on a vertical edge, `^` `v` on a horizontal one) and a circle (a small open ring; a dot saved by an earlier version draws as the circle) to the pen tool's marks on a cell edge. Clicking an edge cycles through the marks ticked in the dialog, each shown with a small picture; the pen tool's own X is one of them. A right click on an edge steps the same cycle backwards (from empty to the last ticked mark, from the X to empty) and keeps the browser's menu closed there; a right click anywhere but on an edge or a grid corner keeps its menu. Touch has no right click, so it only steps forward. **Grid-corner marks:** a click within 0.2 of a cell of a grid corner, inner or outer, cycles an X, then a circle, then empty (a right click goes backwards, and keeps the browser's menu closed there); there are no arrows, since a corner has no edge direction. The dialog's two Corner marks boxes (X and Circle, each with a picture) turn each on or off, and with both off a click near a corner is SudokuPad's own edge click again. A corner shared by four cells is one mark. Corner marks are half the Thickness slider, like edge marks, and are saved in the same place as the edge marks settings (data saved by an earlier version reads as both boxes ticked). These wrap SudokuPad's pen tool and renderer, which are internals, so a SudokuPad update can break them, and the edge and corner marks are then left off, and page 3 lines draw as plain lines with no chevrons; the sliders also rely on SudokuPad's line mode pulldown, so if an update removes it they and the page button do not appear (the saved thickness and opacity still apply, but cannot be changed until this script is updated), while the colors still work. The dialog's Restore defaults goes back to the darkened page (Save keeps it), and sets the dialog's Thickness, Opacity and Dash length sliders and the two Show boxes back to their defaults, all of which wait for Save like the colors (Cancel leaves what was in force). Turn the pen tool on in settings first. It replaces Pen Color and Edge Marks: installed copies of those two stop updating, so remove them by hand in Tampermonkey; your saved colors and edge marks carry over.
- [Dark Page](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caneff/sudokupad-scripts/main/dark-page.user.js): makes the page black around the puzzle, or another color you pick in a settings dialog (a color picker, and a Black button to go back); the choice is kept and applies without a reload, and the page's own text turns dark on a light color. On a dark color the control buttons go dark gray with light purple icons; on a light one they stay as SudokuPad draws them. A rectangle bounding everything the puzzle draws, and the cells you write in, stays as SudokuPad renders it. Not SudokuPad's dark mode.
- [No Tab Letters](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/caneff/sudokupad-scripts/main/no-tab-letters.user.js): stops Tab from toggling SudokuPad's letter mode. SudokuPad binds both `/` and Tab to the letter toggle and saves the mode, so a stray Tab left the number pad on letters after a refresh. This replaces only the Tab binding's handler in the letter tool with one that does nothing, so during play Tab does what it did before minus the toggle (focus stays where it is), the letter button and `/` still toggle, and Tab still moves focus in dialogs. It works whenever you turn the letter tool on in settings. It has no settings entry or dialog. If a SudokuPad update changes what it hooks (the letter tool or its Tab binding to the toggle), it logs a warning in the browser console and changes nothing.

Each script except No Tab Letters adds an entry to SudokuPad's settings, under the Experimental group (collapsed until you open it). The entry shows the script's name; its dialog shows the version in small text beside the heading, so you can tell which one is installed.
