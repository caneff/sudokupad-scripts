# Color by Digit

> [!WARNING]
> **This script can break at any time, and there are no guarantees.** It relies on SudokuPad's internals, which aren't a public API, so any SudokuPad update can break it without notice.

The full reference. For a quick look, see the [details page](https://caneff.github.io/sudokupad-scripts/color-by-digit.html).

In Cell mode, every cell holding a digit, and every empty cell whose center marks all belong to one group, takes its group's color and keeps it in step as you solve. In Digit mode the digits themselves take it instead, in a darker shade, and Both does both. You set the groups in a settings dialog.

- **Profiles** are named lists of groups, one to six of them, with exactly one active. A fresh install has five preloads: Low/5/High (active), Odd/Even, Entropic (0-3, 4-6, 7-9), Modular (1/4/7, 2/5/8, 0/3/6/9) and Each digit (1 to 9 each on its own color, 0 in no group: 5 to 9 on their own keys of the first palette page, and 1 to 4 on purple, pink and brown from the second page and green from the third, keys j, e, h and m of pages 2 and 3, so no digit's shade is black or gray like a given). An install with saved profiles gets Entropic and Modular added once, and Each digit once, each unless a profile already has the same groups or the six profiles are taken; delete one and it stays deleted. The button's icon shows the active profile as **chips**: a run of joined board cells, one per group color (the first three, so Each digit's is no wider than the others), in one outline with single dividers and no gaps or rounded corners. A profile with no groups draws three blank dashed cells. The profile menu and the dialog's profile list show every profile as the same run, drawn as Cell mode draws it; the button draws it in the mode's look. Each cell carries the group's shown digit: with 0 left out, the lowest if every digit is below 5, the highest if every digit is above 5, else the middle (the lower middle on an even count), so Low/5/High shows 1, 5 and 9. A group of only 0 shows 0. With a separate digit profile the button's chips in Digit and Both can show other digits (see the Cell and Digit pulldowns). Groups you saved before profiles existed become the active profile: the preload they match, else a new first profile named Custom, with the five preloads beside it.
- **The profile menu** opens from the arrow box on the button, a right click on it, or a hold on it. Pick a profile and it becomes active at once (with the Digit pulldown on a named profile, see below, each row instead ends in a Cell dot and a Digit dot, under the words Cell and Digit). In Cell and Both the old profile's group colors come off every cell and every cell that holds one group's digit (or has only that group's center marks) gets its new color, as one undo step, which Undo and Redo also take the active profile back and forward with; in Digit and Both the digits take their new shades. Edit profiles… opens the settings dialog, where you reorder profiles by dragging the handle, delete one with ×, and click a row to select it (a row's handle and × show while the pointer is over it, and always on the selected row): the selected row's name is a field you can rename it in (an empty box keeps the old name), and the right column edits its groups by painting. A page pulldown sits above one grid: the palette's swatches on the left and a pad of ten digit keys (1 to 9, 0 under 7) on the right, each digit key filled with its digit's color and blank when the digit is in no group. Click a swatch to make that color the brush (it starts as the color of the selected profile's lowest group, else the first visible color of the current page), then click digit keys to paint them; a click on a digit already in the brush color clears it. You can also press on a digit and drag across others to paint them all, or start the drag on a digit already in the brush color to clear instead. A group is the digits that share a color, so a color with no digits is no group, and the groups run in order of their lowest digit, with 0 after 9. Clear all clears every digit. Groups saved with two entries in one color, which the old group editor allowed, are merged into one when the dialog opens. Selecting a row changes no color and sets no profile: the list is only for editing, and the three pulldowns at the top of the dialog do the picking. + New profile, under the list, adds a copy of the selected profile named after it with " copy", selects it and puts the cursor in its name; it is disabled at six profiles. Save applies every change at once; Cancel drops them.
- **Restore defaults**, in the settings dialog's button row, brings back the five preloads and the Cell and Digit pulldowns' starting picks (the mode stays) as a draft that Cancel drops. It asks first when the profiles in the dialog include one of your own.
- **The Cell and Digit pulldowns**, at the top of the settings dialog, pick the profile that colors the cells (Cell, the active profile) and the one that colors the digits (Digit). Digit starts on **Same as cell** on a fresh install and after an update: one profile colors cells and digits, and a menu pick moves both. Pick a profile by name in Digit (saved as `digitActive`) and there are two picks from the one list, a cell profile for Cell and Both and a digit profile for Digit and Both, and the profile menu shows them as two columns of dots, Cell and Digit; a Cell dot recolors only the cells (one undo step), and a Digit dot only the digits. Picking a name in Digit while it says Same as cell starts the digit profile on the profile you pick; picking Same as cell again makes the digits take the cell profile. The button's chips show the cell profile's groups in Cell and Off and the digit profile's in Digit; in Both they show the digits that reach the most groups of the two profiles. In Both there are as many chips as the larger of the two profiles has groups (three at most), on the set of digits that scores most (the cell groups plus the digit groups its digits fall in); a tie goes to the set sharing most digits with the Cell strip (the chips Cell and Off show), then to the lowest digits. The chips run in ascending digit order, except that Both with one profile on both shows the Cell strip itself. Deleting the digit profile makes the digits follow the cell profile again (Same as cell).
- **Digit: None** drops digit coloring altogether (the saved `useDigitColors`, on by default, turns off): a press steps Cell ↔ Off, the Mode pulldown offers only Cell and Off, the profile menu drops its Cell and Digit dots, and no digit is colored or circled. Picking None while the mode is Digit moves it to Off, in Both to Cell. Pick Same as cell or a profile again and the four modes and the digit profile come back; the mode stays where None moved it. All three pulldowns are drafts: Save applies them, Cancel drops them. Copy settings and Paste settings carry the choice and the digit profile; settings copied before them paste as coloring digits. A paste replaces the draft's profiles with the pasted ones, and the draft's mode stays unless one was pasted; a mode the draft's profiles do not allow (Digit or Both with Digit set to None) falls back to the one they do.
- **The mode** is Cell, Digit, Both or Off, named by the word above the button's chips (CELL, DIGIT, BOTH, OFF, gray when Off), which draw the mode the way it colors the board: Cell fills the cells with their group colors and SudokuPad's blue digits; Digit leaves them white with the digits in their darker shades; Both fills them with the shaded digits; Off draws them white and gray. The button's tooltip names the mode too. A short press on the button steps it, Cell → Digit → Both → Off → Cell, and the settings dialog has the same choice as its Mode pulldown; Save applies a changed mode as a press would. A fresh install starts in Cell; an install that had the old auto mode keeps its look, Cell if auto mode was on and Off if it was off.
- In Cell and Both the cell colors follow the board: a cell that holds one group's digit, or has only center marks of one group, gets that group's color and loses any other group's; any other cell loses every group color. Corner marks are ignored, and colors in no group stay.
- A digit or center mark change and its color change are one undo step. Undo, redo and replays never trigger cell coloring.
- Entering Cell or Both, loading a puzzle in either, and a restart in either color the grid: every cell with a digit or center marks gets its colors, not only the givens, in one undo step. A cell with no digit and no center marks keeps whatever color it has. Saving changed groups in Cell or Both recolors the whole grid in one undo step.
- Leaving Cell or Both for Digit or Off takes every group color of the active profile off every cell, in one undo step; colors in no group stay. A group color you remove by hand in Cell stays off until that cell's digit or center marks change, or until entering Cell, a restart, or a reload in Cell recolors the cell.
- With the Multi-color setting off, SudokuPad replaces a cell's color when a group color is added, so a color in no group stays only with Multi-color on.
- In Digit and Both, entered digits, corner marks and center marks take a darker shade of their group's color, as the palette the puzzle shows has it, the way Pen Tool Extras darkens cell colors for its pens (the OKLCH lightness times 0.75), so a puzzle's own palette gets its own shades. Givens, digits in no group, and digits in a group whose color cannot be read keep SudokuPad's own color. A mark that conflicts keeps its shade and gets a small red circle behind it, while SudokuPad shows conflicts; a conflicting mark of a digit on SudokuPad's own color stays SudokuPad's red and gets the circle too. The shades are a stylesheet, not part of the puzzle, so they are never an undo step; a profile switch or a mode change shows them at once.
